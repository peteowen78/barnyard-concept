Three directions, with the fun put back

Barnyard's current logo is a gang of animals peering over a sign, and that warmth is worth keeping. These three keep the character but draw it properly: one simple, consistent set of animals instead of clip-art, plus copy with a sense of humour.

The idea

Their own logo, grown up. The same gang of animals peeking over the sign, redrawn as one simple matching set, on the yellow people already recognise. Type is Bagel Fat One with DM Sans.

How the fun shows up

The animals become a kit of characters that pop up everywhere: peeking round page edges, on bags, on the van, as stickers for kids at the till.

Why it works

The easiest yes for the owners, because it is clearly still them, only better. The story writes itself: "we kept your animals and drew them properly."

Risk

The least dramatic change of the three.

The idea

Round one's Feed Merchant look, loosened up. The stamped lettering stays, but animals climb over it, stickers get slapped on at angles, and the copy has jokes. Type is Besley with Work Sans.

How the fun shows up

In the contrast: a proper merchant's label that the animals keep interrupting.

Why it works

Speaks to both audiences at once. Horse owners see expertise, dog owners see personality.

Risk

Green and cream is a bigger departure from the yellow they have now.

The idea

Loud, modern and led by words. Each animal gets its own colour and its own noise, and the logo is a speech bubble. Type is Bricolage Grotesque with DM Sans.

How the fun shows up

In big, bold colour blocks and one running joke that works on everything from shop signs to social posts.

Why it works

The most memorable and the most "designed". It makes navigation simple too: pick your animal's colour.

Risk

The furthest from what they have, and the least countryside in feel. Could read as a city pet brand.
