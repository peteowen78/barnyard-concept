Can people find Barnyard online?

We looked at your website the way Google and a first-time visitor do. Here is what we found, in plain English, with no jargon.

6 of 10

Your site passes 6 of the 10 basic checks. The foundations are sound: Google can find you and read what you do. Four gaps are holding the site back, and all four are fixable.

The one that matters most

The most important line on your home page is blank.

Every page has one main heading. It is the first thing a search engine reads to work out what the page is about. On your home page that heading exists, but it is empty.

The words "Barnyard Pet and Equestrian Supplies" do appear, but only as a smaller heading further down. So the strongest signal you can send about who you are is currently saying nothing.

Three smaller things to fix

None of these is hard. Together they decide how you look in search results and when someone shares your link.

To fix

Shared links show no photograph

When someone shares your site on Facebook or WhatsApp, the title and description appear but no picture does. A shop full of animals and a dog play area has plenty worth showing.

A new site would attach a good photograph of the shop to every share.

To fix

Your pictures are labelled with camera file names

Each picture can carry a short written description, used by image searches and read aloud to visitors who cannot see the screen. Of 17 pictures, 6 have none, and the rest carry file names such as "20251025_164900.jpg".

A new site would describe every photograph in plain words: the dog den, the feed barn, the shop front.

To fix

Google is not handed your shop's details

Websites can give Google a small fact sheet: what kind of shop you are, your address, phone number and opening hours. Yours has none, so Google has to piece those together from elsewhere.

A new site would include that fact sheet, so your hours and address come straight from you.

To fix

A summary file for AI tools

Your site already has an AI summary file, made automatically by Wix. It describes the shop well, but its title calls the site "My Vxw Site Qbt3vo", a code left over from when the site was set up.

That title is a quick change in your Wix settings.
